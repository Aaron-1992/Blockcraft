# V6.13: local MIDI listening with note-block sounds

## Behavior

The MIDI compiler menu now has **Play full**, **Play section** and **Stop**, separate from **Build circuit**. Full playback ignores the default five-second build section. Section playback uses Start/Length. Neither listening option calls the physical planner, changes blocks, moves the player, requires loaded terrain along a rail, or sends audio to other players.

The audio device's sample clock schedules the converted notes, including their decaying tails, even while the single-player world is paused. Playback reads the same 16 `SoundEngine` note-block samples used by physical blocks. It uses identical pitch multipliers for 25 pitches per instrument, octave folding, 100ms quantization and a fixed 100ms lead-in; it does not synthesize MIDI sustain, note duration, velocity or pitch bends. Duplicate converted notes are merged/reported. Audio is centered, bypasses distance/occlusion/underwater filtering, follows Sounds volume and lowers background music. Dense overlaps are softly limited for headroom.

Listening bypasses the 256-block rail, 512-note build cap and six physical lanes. Explicit software safety caps: 1 MiB/type 0 or 1 MIDI; 100,000 events; 50,000 converted notes; 60-minute playback windows; 32 distinct notes per 100ms chord. These are implementation limits, not intrinsic note-block pitch rules. Full songs with notes beyond the duration cap are refused, not truncated. Physical building keeps its previous limits.

Keep the menu open to listen. Stop, Close/Escape, field/height/file changes, world changes or loss of compiler access cancel scheduled notes and tails. Restart replaces the old session. A parsed playing song survives deletion of its source file. Playback is local, temporary and not stored as a world program. Device-buffered audio can take a short time to drain after Stop.

The user's DSi Shop file was not supplied, so that particular MIDI is not claimed tested. No physical-device speaker/headphone or two-PC audition is claimed.
